
# Ocean personal website directions

## Shared brief

Ocean is an AI application developer and photographer. The website should let a recruiter or collaborator understand the person, inspect selected work, and move through photography as a way of seeing. The ocean element returns as a personal structure, not as a generic beach theme. Existing photography files are sample images and remain labeled as such until replaced with Ocean's own work.

## Research notes from recent.design

The current Web feed was reviewed on 2026-10-31. The useful patterns are structural rather than decorative:

- [Earth Timeline](https://recent.design/i/l9294qq-earth-timeline) turns a huge subject into one clear live control: a scrubber changes the state of a 3D globe. Lesson: a personal site can have one meaningful timeline or tide control instead of many micro-animations.
- [Portfolio with Walking Avatar](https://recent.design/i/hdhkn18-portfolio-with-walking-avatar) makes the maker part of the portfolio's movement. Lesson: personal identity can be a behavior in the page, not a portrait card.
- [James Clements Personal Site](https://recent.design/i/zpouyfj-james-clements-personal-site) uses a small illustrated stamp and a name-to-domain transition. Lesson: one recurring personal mark is stronger than a collection of generic decorations.
- [The Blue Hour Newspaper](https://recent.design/i/792e5r8-the-blue-hour-newspaper) builds a web page as a living broadsheet with editorial columns, moon phases, and live composition. Lesson: an ocean diary can use field notes, timestamps, and evidence rather than a portfolio card grid.
- [Animated Footer Logo](https://recent.design/i/96clvkf-animated-footer-letterforms) gives one wordmark a precise hover behavior that reveals its construction. Lesson: reserve complex interaction for one branded moment.
- [Gaussian Splat Portal Jumping](https://recent.design/i/a75z6ja-gaussian-splat-portal-jumping) uses portals to move between distinct worlds. Lesson: a transition between making and seeing can be spatial, but the metaphor must stay limited to one action.

## Six candidates

### Best designer candidates

1. **Tidal Index / Irma Boom logic** — a compressed personal archive with changing scale, dates, marginal notes, and one horizontal tide control. The ocean is a way of sorting time and observation.
2. **Coast Manual / Sugiura Kohei logic** — a layered field notebook where photographs, captions, route fragments, and marks build a visual rhythm. The ocean is treated as a material and a memory system.

### Same-medium references

3. **Deep Current / Earth Timeline reference** — a dark, spatial network where projects and photo stories are nodes. The ocean becomes an actual current map for navigation.
4. **Blue Hour Desk / The Blue Hour Newspaper reference** — a browser-native newspaper with a tide bulletin, essays, and selected work. The ocean is present through editorial rhythm, not illustration.

### Seconds-wheel candidates

5. **Walking Shoreline / Portfolio with Walking Avatar reference** — a horizontal project walk with a simple personal silhouette moving through work. The ocean appears as a moving horizon and route.
6. **Portal Coast / Gaussian Splat reference** — a restrained portal between a product case study and a photo story. The ocean is one threshold, not the entire interface.

## Three full directions shown now

### Direction 1 — Tidal Index

Source logic: editorial archive, recent.design Earth Timeline, and the information density of a broadsheet. Borrowed rules: one live horizontal scrubber, date-led annotations, a strong image as evidence, and alternating wide/narrow reading columns. Main material: existing city and coast photographs, explicitly labeled sample images. Skeleton: large left title and image on the first screen, a tide scrubber below, then work and photography as indexed entries. Bold move: the scrubber changes the page's “water level” copy and active image. Form comes from Ocean's actual habit of moving between AI product work and photographs of changing light.

### Direction 2 — Deep Current

Source logic: Earth Timeline's single live control plus Gaussian Splat's portal idea, reduced to a 2D map. Borrowed rules: a dark field, a small number of high-contrast nodes, one central interaction, and detail appearing only when a node is selected. Main material: project names, story titles, and a small set of image crops. Skeleton: an ocean-current map is the home page; selected work appears in a side panel. Bold move: click a current node to change the whole detail panel. Form comes from the relationship between a product workflow, an AI agent, and a photographic sequence.

### Direction 3 — Coast Manual

Source logic: The Blue Hour Newspaper and James Clements' personal stamp behavior. Borrowed rules: paper-like page, editorial columns, one personal mark, short notes with long reading, and a footer that feels like the back of a notebook. Main material: photographs, handwritten-style annotations rendered as text, and a simple tide mark. Skeleton: a vertical “coast manual” with a fixed left index, image contact sheets, and personal notes. Bold move: each section is opened by a redacted field-note line that reveals its image on hover. Form comes from the user's actual mixed practice: making software, observing people, and keeping ordinary visual evidence.

## Why these three are different

Tidal Index is chronological and editorial. Deep Current is spatial and interactive. Coast Manual is tactile and autobiographical. They share the same truthful content but do not share a page skeleton.
